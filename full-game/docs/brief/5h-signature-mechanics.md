# Section 5: signature mechanics

Five mechanics that exist because this world is made of text. They sit on top of the blade-and-word combat, the loot, and the casting in `5a` and `5d`, and replace none of it. Build each one small and exact.

## 1. Close reading

Hold the Read action without a text in focus and the player enters close reading.

- The realistic image falls away and the whole view becomes text at every distance, as set out in 3.11. This is the only way to see the world's writing from afar. Every Inscription in sight is drawn large and upright.
- It shows each target's resistance tier, any word reactions already discovered for that species, and hidden lines the player's Perception can reach.
- In solo play, time slows to about a third while it is held, and it drains Stamina. Attacks, shots, and casts can be aimed and released from it.
- In co-op the shared simulation never slows. The reader's focused target is marked in the partner's view for a few seconds instead, and the Stamina drain is halved.
- It never reveals anything Sealed beyond the fact that it is Sealed.

## 2. Sorts

A sort is a single piece of type. The player finds four punctuation sorts and can set one on each hand's word. A sort changes how the word is delivered, never what it means, and adds to its Ink cost.

| Sort | Name | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `!` | Exclaim | The word bursts onto every valid target within a few metres, at reduced strength |
| `,` | Comma | After landing, the word passes on to one more valid target nearby |
| `...` | Ellipsis | The word lands after a delay set by how long it was charged, so one player can set up a combination alone |
| `?` | Query | Changes nothing. The target gives up what it knows: every word it answers to is added to the bestiary, and any hidden line on it is revealed |

- Each sort is a unique object found once. The Query is given in the prologue's last room. The other three are placed at points of interest named in `docs/SCOPE.md`.
- Sorts work with words from a hand or a Staff, and with words carried by arrows.
- The Emendation matrix test covers every word with every sort.

## 3. Typos

Words can go wrong, and going wrong is a second spellbook. Saint Slip is the patron of this.

- There are 32 typos among the 35 words. Each word has at most one **typo**, a real neighbouring word with its own small effect, listed in `docs/bible/08-words-items-craft.md`.
- A typo fires in two ways. By accident: being hit while holding a full charge smudges the word into its typo. On purpose: a player who types the typo itself, on a keyboard or the letter wheel, casts it deliberately.
- A lesser charge that is hit is simply lost, as in `5a`.
- Typos cost the Ink of the word they came from, obey resistance tiers, never affect Sealed words, and fade quickly.
- The first time a typo fires, it is recorded on the journal's **Errata** page with what it did. Nothing in the game tells the player that typos can be cast on purpose. Books hint at it.
- A typo may set an ordinary state as a side effect, such as `CRY` leaving its target `WET`, so typos join the systemic chain and can complete a compound.
- No typo may complete, block, or break a quest. The validator requires every typo to have a result or a refusal for every target class.

## 4. The Remembered

The Erasure takes memory. The Unentered is the only one who keeps it, and cannot keep all of it.

- When the Erasure takes something, a list of what is being forgotten opens on the journal's **Remembered** page: people, places, and small things. The player may keep three from each list. The rest fade from the page as they watch.
- The first list follows Little Penance in Q3. One more follows each of the three Erasure stages.
- A kept entry can be **read back** once, at the place it belonged to or to a person it concerned. Each has one short authored outcome: a widow who knows her husband's name again, a shrine that returns as its Verso impression, a debt that stands after all.
- Reading back never restores what the main story has removed, and never changes a script line that is already voiced.
- Each list offers about eight candidates, so there are about 32 authored outcomes in the game.
- The epilogue names what the player kept and what they let go.
- In co-op, both players must agree on each choice. The second player is the Witness, and co-signs.

## 5. An earned name

No one can write the player's name, so the world writes what they did.

- The game counts deeds of many kinds: gates opened, debts paid for others, words mistyped, graves read, goats offended.
- From about 40 authored epithets it holds the two the player has most earned, such as "who opened the Tollway" or "of the many typos". These change as play changes.
- Epithets are descriptions and never a name. They appear in the generated text on the player's clothing and gear, in guards' and merchants' greetings, on the journal's cover, on a grave marker if the player dies, and in the epilogue.
- In co-op each player earns their own.
