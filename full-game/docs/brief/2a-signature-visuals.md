# Section 2: signature visuals

Four visual ideas that only a world written in fine print can show. Each must obey Section 3: glyphs stay anchored to surfaces, and nothing here may add visible text, blur, or noise to the realistic frame beyond the reveal distance, except where this file says so.

## 1. Typographic body language

Within fighting distance, a creature's letters show its state. In close reading they show it at any distance.

| State | How its letters look |
|---|---|
| Hostile, attacking | Its loud set: capitals and heavy characters |
| Calm, asleep, unaware | Its quiet set: lowercase and light characters |
| Afraid | Gaps open between its letters, and they tremble |
| Wounded | Letters are missing, as the damage mask already shows |
| Near death | Only punctuation is left |
| Rewritten by the player | The changed part of its body flashes the gold of the word, visibly even at a distance, then settles |

- Every species defines a loud set and a quiet set within its glyph family in the bestiary. A change of state is a change of the underlying text, so it is allowed under rule 3.4.
- State must never rely on letters alone. Posture, animation, sound, and the Inscription carry the same information, and reading mode states it in words.

## 2. The Erasure unravels

Everywhere else the world's writing is too fine to see. The Erasure is where it fails, and so it is the one place the text shows from afar.

1. At the edge, surfaces lose colour and fine detail, and their letters grow coarser, as if the world were being printed at a lower resolution.
2. Surface text loses its vowels.
3. Words shorten to their first letters.
4. Only punctuation remains, large and far apart.
5. Bare paper.

- Coarse cascade levels may be drawn here in normal play. Seen from the Vale, the Erasure is a pale band whose edge is faintly, wrongly, made of marks.
- The same order applies to sound: ambience loses its high detail, then its rhythm, then everything but the player's own steps.
- The HUD thins with it, one stage behind the world. Health, Stamina, and Ink never become unreadable, and an accessibility option turns HUD thinning off.
- The Unentered leaves **footprints of text**. Each step prints a fragment of what stood on that spot before it was scraped, read from the chunk's original text pages, in letters large enough to read standing. The footprints fade slowly behind the player. The Witness leaves them too.

## 3. Weather as typography

- **Wind that reads.** Now and then a gust crossing a field of grass or barley bends the stalks in a pattern that, for a second, is handwriting: a passing sentence several metres tall, made of nothing but the lean of real grass. It is gone before the player is sure. Sentences come from a small authored pool for each Quire, in the Ledger's voice. It should be rare enough to make a player stop walking.
- **Rain** looks like rain. Where it strikes close by, each splash is a `'`, it runs down a near wall as `|`, and the puddle at the player's feet ripples in `~`.
- **Snow** settles on ledges and buries wall text. The player can brush it off to read what is beneath. Built with the Whitemarch.
- **Fog** looks like fog. Surfaces close by inside it show gaps where letters have lifted out. Built with the Blotmere.

## 4. Travel through a letter

The title sequence dives through letters within letters. Reuse that dive wherever the player crosses from one place to another. It is the moment the game shows what its world is made of.

- **Doors.** Entering an interior pushes into the door's surface until its fine print fills the view, then through one letter of its Inscription. It takes under a second, affects only the player entering, and replaces the ink wash.
- **Fast travel.** The camera falls into a character on the map at the destination's Bookmark and emerges standing there.
- **Torn Pages.** Entering the Verso passes through the letter and comes out the back of it, mirrored.
- With reduced motion on, all three become a plain fade.
