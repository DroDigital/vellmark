# Peoples and daily life

## The three peoples

All three are human. The differences are of history and habit, with a few inherited traits. Skin tone, hair, and build vary widely within each.

| People | Homeland | Traits | Starting bonus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Valefolk** | Scrivenhold Vale, Saltmargin | Town-dwellers, traders, and litigators who can recite their tax status from memory | Speech and Smithing bonuses; better prices |
| **Marchers** | The Whitemarch, Cinder Folio | Tall highlanders, with ink tattoos recording family debts | Cold resistance; Weapons bonus; more Stamina |
| **Foxen** | The Foxing Wood, the Blotmere | Marked with rust-coloured flecks that spread with age, as on old paper. Long memories | Sneak and Perception bonuses; more Ink |

## Faith

People honour all six Authors and pray to whichever seems least busy. Shrines hold a page of the Author's marginalia, usually forged. Common practices:

- **Reading in:** a newborn's name is read into the local Lesser Ledger, then a chip of bread is thrown over the left shoulder for Saint Slip.
- **Reading out:** at a funeral, the dead person's entry is read aloud, and everyone argues about the facts.
- **Thumb-blotting:** touching your thumb to your tongue and then your chest, to ask pardon for an error.
- **Swearing:** "Gall's teeth," "by the red pen," "scrape it," "blot and blast," and "the Gilder's own receipt."

## Law and government

- The **Lord Registrar** rules from Scrivenhold on behalf of the Great Ledger. The realm has no monarch.
- Each Quire has a **Sheriff of the Page**, who collects tithes and hears disputes. Many have become petty tyrants.
- **Crime** is a debt. A bounty is literally an outstanding balance. Guards carry tally boards and are politer than you might expect until the moment they aren't.
- **Lesser Ledgers** are local copies that are kept in town halls, and they are the basis of property, marriage, and inheritance.

## Calendar

Twelve months, called Gatherings: Thaw, Seed, Sedge, Hay, Lammas, Reap, Harrow, Tithe, Fog, Frost, Longnight, and Audit. Weeks have seven days with their ordinary names, and it is unlucky to begin a lawsuit on a Tuesday.

## Money

The currency is the **Mark**, a small stamped lead-and-silver token with a tiny ledger line on the reverse. Prices are quoted in Marks.

| Item | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Bread and ale | 2 Marks |
| A night at an inn | 15 Marks |
| A decent sword | 200 Marks |
| A cottage | 5,000 Marks |

## Food and drink

- **Vale:** barley bread, onion pies, small beer, and arguments.
- **Whitemarch:** smoked goat, fermented milk, and a spirit called Long Division.
- **Foxing Wood:** mushroom loaf, chestnut paste, and sour cider.
- **Saltmargin:** salt herring, seaweed cake, and rum made from things no one lists.
- **Blotmere:** eel, reed-honey, and bog tea, which can also be used to strip paint.
- **Cinder Folio:** ash-baked roots, peppered mutton, and hot wine.

## Customs worth using

- It is rude to read anyone's clothing at close range without permission.
- Children learn to read at four, because the world is covered in writing.
- Bad neighbours are said to "have a thick margin."
- A person who owes nobody anything is called "paid up," and it is also a polite term for the dead.
- Every household keeps a **Remembering Shelf** with objects that belonged to the forgotten dead. In recent years, many people have started finding items on theirs they cannot explain.
- Wedding vows include the phrase "and I will correct you gently."

## Reactions to the Unentered

NPCs find the player's lack of an entry unsettling. Common reactions:

- Clerks become distressed and try to fill out a form anyway.
- Dogs like you. Dogs have never cared about paperwork.
- Children ask blunt questions.
- Priests touch their chests and avoid looking at you.
- Bartenders ask for payment up front.
- The Margin Folk offer you a job immediately.

## Common idioms

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Written and witnessed" | Definitely true |
| "That's a footnote" | It doesn't matter |
| "Gone to the gutter" | Ruined |
| "He's a bad copy of his father" | Self-explanatory, and often said to his face |
| "Ink's not dry" | It might still change |
| "Who's reading?" | Who is in charge? |
| "Mind your own entry" | Mind your own business |
